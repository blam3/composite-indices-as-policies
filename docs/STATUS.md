## 2. Verified results — do not re-derive these

All from ACS 2018–2022 5-year Variance Replicate Tables, summary level 140, Louisiana (state FIPS 22), validated against the published CDC/ATSDR SVI 2022 Louisiana file. **Numbers are from the 2026-08-19 post-audit rerun.** Method detail lives in `claude/wp1-svi-reproduction-louisiana-findings.md`; code in §5. **One conflict with that document is open — see the 2026-08-23 changelog entry below — so treat the GROUPQ and NOVEH SE ratios as unconfirmed until resolved against `EXPECTED-RESULTS.json`.**

**Reproduction fidelity**
- **57,368 of 57,368** published values reproduce exactly — all `EP_`, `EPL_`, `SPL_`, `RPL_` fields for all **1,379** tracts — and **57,918 of 57,918** cells agree on missing-versus-present. Both halves are enforced with a nonzero exit.
- The VRT files carry 1,388 tracts; CDC drops the 9 water-only tracts (code `9900xx`).
- **Three** CDC conventions had to be reverse-engineered: SAS-style rounding half away from zero, the `(min-rank − 1)/(n_valid − 1)` tie-low percentile rule, and re-quantisation between summation and ranking. **11 of the 16** `EP_` variables are documented as read off already-rounded published `DP`/`S` percentage fields — display-rounding inheritance is specification, not inference.
- The successive-difference-replication variance estimator reproduces the Census-published `SE` column exactly to integer rounding for every cell with SE ≥ 50.

**Decision reliability (B = 5,000 draws, replicate design)**
- Median SD of a tract's overall percentile: **0.0898**. Median 90% interval width: **29 percentile points**. 80.8% of tracts exceed a 20-point interval; 14.9% exceed 40 points.
- Top decile: 137 tracts funded; **expected leavers 34.2 (24.9% of the published list)**, expected entrants 34.2, total label changes 68.4. The 2026-08-18 figure of "68.4 (49.9%)" was the symmetric difference divided by the funded count and is **withdrawn**. 109 of 137 have a 90% interval crossing the cutoff; 99 have P(funded) < 0.90; 120 unfunded tracts have P(funded) > 0.10.
- Top quintile leaver rate 18.8%; top quartile 15.9%.
- No-covariance simulation ("independent MOE") matches the replicates at the median but understates SD by >25% for 14.9% of tracts and puts 5.0% of tracts in a >40-point interval where the replicates find 14.9%. **It is optimistic, not conservative.**
- **The mechanism was misattributed and is corrected.** Removing *cross-tract* covariance alone changes nothing (median SD 0.0897 vs 0.0898; >40-point fraction 15.4% vs 14.9%). The tail comes entirely from *within-tract* covariance.
- **Robustness.** The 80 published replicates with no Gaussian step give median SD 0.0892 against the model's 0.0898 (ratio 1.006, cross-tract correlation 0.93). Relaxing the boundary clamps raises the leaver rate from 24.9% to 25.4%; clamping fires less often in high-poverty tracts (0.76 Q5/Q1), so it is not manufacturing the subgroup null.

**Subgroup-conditional reliability** — descriptive Louisiana 2022 associations, county-clustered (CR1) standard errors and a draw-block bootstrap; no p-values.
- Input precision *is* regressive: SE ratios, highest- vs lowest-poverty quintile — GROUPQ 2.62, NOVEH 2.51, UNEMP 2.39, CROWD 2.38, POV150 2.22, NOHSDP 2.19, SNGPNT 1.92. `MINRTY` is flat (0.85).
- Rank noise is **not** regressive at the overall percentile. Rank position alone explains R² = 0.59 of rank SD (inverted U, peaking mid-distribution). Conditional on distance to the cutoff, higher-poverty tracts flip *slightly less* (OR 0.88 per SD, clustered t = −2.5).
- **Experimental rather than correlational.** Equalising every tract's input precision to the cross-tract median leaves the inverted-U shape intact (decile profiles correlate 0.93) but cuts rank noise in the **top decile by 36%** and drops the top-decile leaver rate from 24.9% to **20.2%**. The published funded set carries **1.57×** the rank noise it would carry under equal input precision. About a fifth of the churn in the funded set is attributable to unequal input precision.
- Partial correlation between input imprecision and rank SD, holding rank position fixed: **0.485** (an association; the adjustment is close to conditioning on a mediator).

**Second state: Wyoming (FIPS 56), 2026-11-26.** Same chain, only `STATE_FIPS`/`STATE_NAME` changed; numbers from `analysis/wyoming/results/EXPECTED-RESULTS.json`, detail in `docs/wp1-wyoming-findings.md`.
- **6,678 of 6,678** values and **6,720 of 6,720** missingness cells reproduce exactly, all **160** tracts (159 with a valid `RPL_THEMES`; no water-only tracts). No new CDC convention was needed. Wyoming's only missingness is one zero-population tract, so the zero-denominator rule is exercised only trivially.
- Median rank SD **0.1113**; median 90% interval **36 points**; 86.2% of tracts exceed 20 points and **35.2%** exceed 40 points.
- Top decile 16 funded: expected leavers **4.26 (26.6%)**, entrants 4.25, label changes 8.51. Top quintile 20.1%; top quartile 20.3% — the leaver rate does not fall from quintile to quartile.
- Cross-tract null replicates (median SD 0.1108 without it). Within-tract covariance matters more than in Louisiana: `no_cov` understates the median SD by 10% (0.0996), not only the tail. The 80 raw replicates give 0.1175 (ratio 0.947), so the Gaussian model is mildly optimistic here.
- Input precision regressive for 14 of 16 inputs (POV150 2.59, GROUPQ 2.78, MUNIT 2.54, NOVEH 2.09); `MINRTY` 1.21, not flat. Rank noise not regressive (covariates R² 0.042). Rank position alone R² 0.651; partial correlation of imprecision with rank SD given position **0.542**.
- Equalisation: decile profiles correlate 0.950; top decile carries **1.52×** the rank noise it would under equal precision; matched leaver rate 27.1% → **22.5%**, about a sixth of top-decile churn.

**Louisiana comparators now in the manifest.** The decile profile, the top-decile noise ratio (**1.567**, the "1.57×" above) and the matched `ep_actual`/`ep_equalised` leaver rates (25.0% → **20.2%**) were in the logs only; `11_expected_results.R` now emits them. No existing manifest field changed.

**The WP1 claim, as it now stands.** The percentile-ranking step launders heterogeneous input precision into near-homogeneous rank noise. Two tracts at the same published percentile are treated identically by every program using the index, but one's position rests on estimates carrying two to three times the sampling error of the other's — and the index publishes no uncertainty on `RPL_THEMES` at all. The equalisation experiment puts a number on the allocation consequence: a fifth of top-decile churn.

## 3. Open decisions

| # | Decision | Why it matters | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Whether the WP1 paper is one-state or multi-state | A single state is a case study, several is a claim about the index. **2026-11-26: the second state is done — Wyoming reproduces exactly and every Louisiana qualitative result transports (quarter-of-the-top-decile churn, regressive input precision with non-regressive rank noise, an inverted U that survives equalisation), with a higher noise level and a pre-registered rank-position prediction not confirmed. This bears on the decision; it does not close it.** The prompt pack proposed Wyoming (FIPS 56) on the coarse-rank-spacing argument. The rank map is a functional of the *reference set*, so state-ranked and US-ranked SVI are different policies over the same tracts. **New, 2026-08-23:** the LIHTC anchor sharpens this — QCT designation is relative to the *metropolitan area*, a third reference set again, so the program now has three live ranking populations and the choice is partly about which policy is being audited. The EWM regret bound scaling as √(VC(Π)/n) still argues for a wider target — illustrative scaling only, not a §2 number. | researcher |
| 5 | Which WP3 formalism replaces the empirical-quantile threshold: a population-quantile rule with an estimated cutoff, or Kitagawa & Tetenov's capacity-constrained formulation | A rank-threshold rule is a function of a tract's covariates *and the sample*, so it is not an individualized treatment rule and the VC regret bound does not apply as written. **Substantially informed 2026-08-23 by the anchor choice, and the answer now looks like "both, conditionally."** QCT designation has a sharp statutory eligibility test (≥50% of households under 60% AMGI, *or* poverty rate ≥25%) **and** a hard cap: designated QCTs may not exceed 20% of a metropolitan area's population. When the cap does not bind, QCT is a clean threshold rule and the RD-flavoured reading holds. When it binds, HUD ranks eligible tracts and designates down the list until the cap is reached — which is Kitagawa & Tetenov's capacity constraint with *deterministic rank-order rationing* rather than random rationing among the eligible. That is the same structure as SVI's top-decile rule, so the tension flagged when this decision was opened is a feature of the real programme, not an artifact of the formalism. **2026-10-05:** a literature scan surfaced Cerulli (2026, arXiv:2605.12235) on policy learning under a *joint* budget-and-coverage constraint, with an affine dual-shadow-price threshold characterization that may reconcile the two candidate formalisms rather than forcing a choice — not yet assessed by the researcher. **2026-11-23:** a further scan surfaced Bong (2026, arXiv:2611.14572) on strategic causal policy learning with manipulable eligibility — threshold corrections under "pointwise safety" and minimax group-welfare-regret criteria — tangential (its mechanism is strategic covariate manipulation, not capacity-constrained rank-order rationing) but worth a skim when mapping this space; not yet assessed. Remaining judgment: whether to model the binding and non-binding metros under one formalism or treat cap-binding as the identifying variation. | researcher |

The substance, in three lines. Linearising SVI's four ranking stages gives `R̂_i − R_i ≈ w̃_i'η_i` with `w̃_ij` a product of the local densities at each stage — so **CDC's nominal unit weights vanish from the rank-scale noise entirely**, and what weights an indicator at the decision is how crowded the tract's neighbourhood is on that indicator. The realised assignment probability becomes `Φ((r−τ)/ς(r))` with group entering only through `H_g`, the distribution of group membership over rank; group churn is that group's density at the cutoff times the local noise scale, and nothing else. The group effect re-enters through three channels, one of which — the rank map being a functional of the reference set — was not in the researcher's stated guess and is the more consequential: state-ranked and US-ranked SVI are different policies over the same tracts, which gives §6.4(iii)'s transport problem a measurement-layer twin unrelated to outcome-model shift.
